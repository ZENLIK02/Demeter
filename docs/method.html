Expiring Mars

Method, data sources, limits and AI use.

Map Method & data

The question

Space agencies and rover builders test equipment at Earth sites chosen because they resemble Mars: dry, barren and cold. Those sites were picked decades ago, and Earth's climate is changing. We ask how often each site now leaves the conditions that made it Mars-like.

How we measure it

  1. Each site's own normal. For every site, variable and calendar month, we take the monthly values from the baseline years and set a threshold at the 90th percentile (never less than a small minimum step above the median, so near-zero noise at hyperarid sites does not count).
  2. Excursion months. A month is an excursion when the value passes that threshold in the direction that is not Mars-like: wetter, warmer, or wetter soil.
  3. Decade comparison. We compare excursion months per year in the baseline years with the recent years. A variable is "drifting" when the rate rises by at least 0.5 months per year. A site is "drifting" if any variable drifts, and "leaving Mars-like conditions" if half or more do.

Every number is shown per variable. We do not hide them inside one combined score.

Does the method catch a real event?

Yes. In 2015 and 2017 rare rains fell on the hyperarid core of the Atacama Desert, and researchers documented a die-off of the local microbes afterwards (Azua-Bustos et al., 2018). Our index flags rainfall excursions at the Yungay site in March 2015 and June 2017, with no tuning to that event. This check runs automatically with every code change.

Limits you should know

  • Satellites and models measure the environment, not biology. We do not measure microbes or instrument performance.
  • Coarse resolution. The current data grid is about 50 km, far larger than a test area. Local conditions can differ.
  • Polar sites. Satellite rainfall is unreliable beyond about 60° latitude, so polar sites rely on reanalysis data, which is model-based.
  • Warming shows up everywhere. Air temperature excursions rose at most sites. That is real, but it means temperature alone does not single out a site.
  • Intermittent, not only trending. Desert rain comes in rare bursts. A site can be non-Mars-like for a season and then recover.
  • Earth is never Mars. No Earth site reproduces Mars's low gravity, thin atmosphere or radiation. We only track the properties that made each site a useful analog.
  • Scope. This version covers Mars analogs, not Moon analogs.

Use of AI

We used an AI coding assistant (Claude by Anthropic) to help write and review the data pipeline, this website and its documentation. The analysis itself uses no machine learning: every result comes from the rule-based method above, and the team checked the outputs against the source data. Any AI-generated audio or imagery in our demo video is declared on the project page.
